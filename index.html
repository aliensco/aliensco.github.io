About Yuhan Li

I am an undergraduate researcher at the School of Intelligent Systems Engineering, Sun Yat-sen University. I am pursuing dual bachelor's degrees in Intelligent Science and Technology and Business Administration, alongside a minor in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics.

My research interests lie in multimodal foundation models, embodied intelligence, causal video world models, and trustworthy knowledge reasoning. I am passionate about building AI systems that do more than produce plausible outputs: I want them to form grounded, causal, and verifiable models of the real world.

Mathematics is the theoretical foundation of how I approach artificial intelligence. I enjoy moving from precise abstractions to testable models, using mathematical reasoning to study learning, optimization, uncertainty, physical dynamics, and causality. My business training helps me connect research ideas with real users, useful products, and long-term impact.

I am always interested in research conversations and collaborations around world models, embodied AI, graph reasoning, and reliable AI systems.

Research Interests

  • Causal video world models: learning physical dynamics, counterfactual outcomes, and temporally consistent causal event chains from video.
  • Embodied and multimodal intelligence: connecting visual perception, language, action, and physical interaction in real environments.
  • Trustworthy knowledge reasoning: evidence-grounded retrieval, temporal conflict resolution, graph reasoning, and verifiable question answering.
  • Mathematical foundations of AI: optimization, uncertainty, causal modeling, and principled analysis of learning systems.

Selected Projects

T2K: Thread-to-Knowledge

Research & Engineering · Ongoing

Knowledge Reasoning

A knowledge production engine for the Yeegoods campus platform that transforms community threads, reviews, products, services, and web content into structured, verifiable knowledge. T2K extracts atomic claims and evidence spans, normalizes entities, aspects, time, and conditions, and models support, conflict, update, and similarity relations.

Human review, provenance tracking, and graph verification keep downstream retrieval and question answering grounded in traceable evidence.

EverydayBifurcationBench

Project Lead & First Author · 2025 — Present

World Models

A benchmark for evaluating whether video generation models can understand and reproduce physical causality in realistic everyday scenes. The project constructs controllable, paired counterfactual scenarios around sliding, toppling, support failure, passage constraints, edge falling, and collision propagation.

Event-level metrics diagnose where a model first departs from a valid causal chain and measure its ability to generalize from simulation to real-world physical dynamics.
